## VIDEO 1: What is RFM and Why It Matters (Quick Explainer)

**Title:** RFM Segmentation in 90 Seconds

**Hook (0-3 sec):**
"If you sell anything online or in distribution, you're leaving money on the table right now. Here's why."

**Body (3-50 sec):**
"RFM stands for three things your customers do:

**R** = Recency. When did they last buy? Last week = hot. Three months ago = cold.

**F** = Frequency. How often do they buy? Bought 20 times = loyal. Bought once = risky.

**M** = Monetary. How much do they spend? High spend + high frequency = your best customer. High spend + no recent activity = at risk.

Now here's the power: put these three things together and you've instantly segmented your entire customer base into five groups: Champions, Loyals, Potential, Needing Attention, and At-Risk.

Each group needs a different strategy. Champions get VIP treatment. At-Risk get win-back offers. But 90% of businesses treat every customer the same way. That's why they leave money on the table."

**CTA (50-60 sec):**
"If you've got 1,000+ customers, you need RFM. Comment RFM and I'll send you the free segmentation template."

**Visual Cues:**
- On-screen text: "R = Recency" / "F = Frequency" / "M = Monetary"
- Show three customer journey lines (one hot, one warm, one cold)
- End slide: Five colored boxes for each segment
- Text overlay: "Champion | Loyal | Potential | Needing Attention | At-Risk"

**Tone:** Educational, no fluff, treats viewer as business owner with real problems

**Hook (0-3 sec):**
"Your customer just silently entered a churn pattern. You won't notice for 90 days. But if you knew what to look for, you'd catch it this week."

**Body (3-60 sec):**
"Here's what churn looks like in data:

Three months ago, this customer bought every two weeks. Last month: once. This month: nothing yet.

That's not 'they're busy.' That's a pattern.

Frequency dropped 80%. Recency is now 60+ days. Monetary is trending down. In RFM terms: they moved from Loyal to At-Risk.

Most businesses miss this because they're not looking. They're not segmenting.

Here's how to catch it: pull your customer data every 30 days. Run RFM. Look for these specific movements:

- Customers who were Loyal → now At-Risk
- Customers with declining frequency
- High-value customers with extended recency

When you spot this pattern, you intervene. You don't wait six months. You reach out now. Offer a win-back discount. Ask what's wrong. Save the relationship.

The math: if you save just 3 mid-tier customers per quarter, that's $40K+ you keep that competitors would lose."

"Have you spotted your churn pattern yet? Comment CHURN and I'll send you the 5-minute churn detection checklist."

**Visual Cues:**
- Show a simple line graph with frequency dropping over time (3 downward slopes)
- Text: "Was: Every 2 weeks | Now: Every 8 weeks | Recency: 60+ days"
- Red flags popping up on screen as patterns are mentioned
- Color coding: Green (Loyal) → Orange (Needing Attention) → Red (At-Risk)

**Tone:** Urgent but not panicky. This is about prevention, not emergency.

## VIDEO 6: What Champions, Loyals, and At-Risk Mean in Your Business

"Your customer base has five types. Each one needs a completely different strategy. Most businesses treat them all the same way."

**Loyals:** Also frequent, decent value, been with you 1+ years. Solid. Your job: maintain. Regular engagement, standard loyalty program, proactive customer service. They're not going anywhere if you don't neglect them.

**At-Risk:** High value but declining. This is your emergency segment. Your job: save them now. Personal outreach, VIP discount, direct call. It's cheaper to save them than replace them. If they leave, your revenue just dropped."

## VIDEO 8: The Biggest Data Mistake E-Commerce Companies Make

"I've analyzed 50 e-commerce businesses. They all make the same data mistake. And it's costing them 10-15% of revenue."

**Body (3-60 sec):**
"Here's the mistake:

They're mixing B2B transactions with B2C transactions in their segmentation.

Think about it: an order from a wholesale buyer is not the same as an order from a consumer. Size is different. Frequency is different. Decision-making is different. Seasonality is different. Profitability is different.

But most e-commerce systems treat them identically. Same segmentation logic. Same retention strategy. Same pricing.

Then they wonder why their retention rate is weird. Why their LTV calculations are off. Why their marketing ROI looks inconsistent.

Here's what happens when you separate them:

B2C segment: higher volume, lower value per order, buying for themselves. Focus on repeat purchase and brand loyalty.

B2B segment: lower volume, higher value per order, buying for resale. Focus on bulk discounts, payment terms, and reliability.

One company I worked with separated these. Their B2B retention strategy completely changed. They added payment terms, volume tiers, and account-based marketing. B2B revenue increased 24%. B2C stayed the same but became more profitable because messaging was focused.

By trying to optimize for both, they were optimizing for neither."

Now, how much of your customer service resources go to those 200? Probably 20% (equally distributed).

How much of your retention budget? Same. Equally distributed.

How much personalization? Same. Generic.

That's the mistake.

Your top 20% should get:

- Dedicated account management
- Personalized pricing and offers
- VIP customer service (shortest response time, highest priority)
- Early access to new products
- Exclusive loyalty rewards
- Direct line to leadership

Cost to implement this? Maybe 10-15% extra spend on that segment.

ROI? Retention increases from 85% to 94%. Average order value increases 12% because they feel special. Lifetime value goes up 40%.

Now take your bottom 20% (the customers generating minimal revenue). Consider:

- Are they profitable after acquisition cost?
- Are you spending more to service them than they generate?
- Would it be better to let them go and redirect that spend?

One brand did this. Fired the bottom 15% of customers (in terms of profitability). Reinvested that service cost into the top 30%. Within six months, top 30% revenue was up 18%, overall profit was up 24%."
