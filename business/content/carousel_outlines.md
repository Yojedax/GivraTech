## CAROUSEL 1: "The RFM Framework: How to Segment Your Customers in 5 Steps"

**Title (Cover Slide):**
How to Segment 10,000 Customers in One Spreadsheet

---

### SLIDE 1 (Cover Slide)
**Headline:** The RFM Framework: How to Segment Your Customers in 5 Steps

**Body Text:**
If you have 1,000+ customers, you need this framework. It takes one spreadsheet and five simple steps. No software required.

---

### SLIDE 2: What is RFM?
**Headline:** RFM = Three Questions About Your Customers

**Body Text:**
R = Recency: When did they last buy?
F = Frequency: How often do they buy?
M = Monetary: How much do they spend?

Answer these three questions about each customer, and you've segmented your entire database. That's it.

---

### SLIDE 3: Why It Works
**Headline:** RFM Works Because It Mirrors Reality

**Body Text:**
Your best customers are recent, frequent, and high-spend. Your worst customers are distant, infrequent, and low-spend. RFM just quantifies what you already know intuitively. It turns gut feeling into data.

---

### SLIDE 4: The Five Segments You'll Create
**Headline:** RFM Creates Five Distinct Customer Types

**Body Text:**
Champions: Recent, frequent, high-spend. Your core business.
Loyals: Frequent and decent spend. Stable base.
Potential: Used to buy, not recently. Growth opportunity.
Needing Attention: Past buyers, now quiet. Dormant potential.
At-Risk: High value, declining engagement. Save them now.

---

### SLIDE 5: Step 1 - Gather Your Data
**Headline:** Step 1: Pull Your Transaction Data

**Body Text:**
Export from your database or CSV: Customer ID, Purchase Date, Purchase Amount. You need at least 12 months of history. Don't worry if it's messy. We'll clean it.

Open a spreadsheet. You're ready.

---

### SLIDE 6: Step 2 - Calculate Recency
**Headline:** Step 2: Calculate Recency (Days Since Last Purchase)

**Body Text:**
For each customer, calculate: Today's Date - Last Purchase Date = Recency in Days.

A customer who bought 5 days ago has Recency of 5.
A customer who bought 200 days ago has Recency of 200.

Lower recency is better.

---

### SLIDE 7: Step 3 - Calculate Frequency
**Headline:** Step 3: Calculate Frequency (Total Number of Purchases)

**Body Text:**
For each customer, count: How many times did they purchase in the last 12 months?

A loyal customer: 24 purchases (bi-weekly)
A casual customer: 3 purchases
A one-timer: 1 purchase

Higher frequency is better.

---

### SLIDE 8: Step 4 - Calculate Monetary
**Headline:** Step 4: Calculate Monetary (Total Revenue Per Customer)

**Body Text:**
For each customer, sum: Total dollars spent in the last 12 months.

A big spender: $12,000
An average customer: $1,500
A small purchase: $200

Higher monetary is better. You can also use average order value instead if that's more relevant.

---

### SLIDE 9: Step 5 - Score and Segment
**Headline:** Step 5: Score Each Customer (Simple Ranking)

**Body Text:**
Create a simple scoring logic:

High Recency (0-30 days) = 5 points
High Frequency (10+ purchases) = 5 points
High Monetary ($2,000+) = 5 points

Each customer gets a score 0-15. Then segment:

15 = Champion
12-14 = Loyal
9-11 = Potential
6-8 = Needing Attention
0-5 = At-Risk

---

### SLIDE 10: What You Do With This
**Headline:** Now You Know Exactly How to Treat Each Segment

**Body Text:**
Champions: VIP programs, exclusive access, premium support.
Loyals: Maintain engagement, loyalty rewards, personalized offers.
Potential: Cross-sell, upsell, educational content.
Needing Attention: Re-engagement campaigns, special offers.
At-Risk: Urgent win-back, personal outreach, retention offers.

Same effort, completely different strategies.

All from better segmentation. Not from new products or services. From smarter strategy.

## CAROUSEL 2: "5 Data Errors That Are Costing Your Business Money Right Now"

---

### SLIDE 2: Error #1 - Duplicate Records
**Headline:** Error #1: Duplicate Customer Records

**Body Text:**
One customer entered three times. Three email variations. Three phone number formats. Your system thinks they're three different people.

Result: Segmentation is fragmented. You're counting them as three customers (inflating your customer count). Marketing spend gets wasted on the same person.

Fix: Run a deduplication script. Merge records. Takes 60 minutes.

---

### SLIDE 3: Error #2 - Unmerged B2B/B2C
**Headline:** Error #2: Mixing B2B and B2C Customers

**Body Text:**
A wholesale buyer gets the same segmentation logic as a consumer. But they're completely different.

B2B: Higher order value, longer sales cycle, needs credit terms, seasonal.
B2C: Lower order value, shorter cycle, impulse-driven, different seasonality.

Treating them the same means both segments get mediocre strategy.

Fix: Tag all customers B2B or B2C. Segment separately. Takes 2 hours.

---

### SLIDE 4: Error #3 - Bad Date Formatting
**Headline:** Error #3: Inconsistent Date Formats

**Body Text:**
Some purchase dates are "2024-01-15". Some are "01/15/2024". Some are "Jan 15, 2024".

Your system can't read them consistently. Recency calculations fail. Sorting breaks. Analysis becomes unreliable.

Fix: Standardize all dates to one format (YYYY-MM-DD). Takes 30 minutes.

---

### SLIDE 5: Error #4 - Invalid Contact Information
**Headline:** Error #4: Unvalidated Email and Phone Data

**Body Text:**
15% of email addresses are wrong (typos, inactive accounts, invalid formats).
8% of phone numbers are formatted inconsistently or invalid.

Result: Email campaigns bounce. SMS can't deliver. You can't reach 23% of your customer base.

Fix: Run validation script on emails and phones. Remove invalid. Takes 45 minutes.

---

### SLIDE 6: Error #5 - Incomplete Records
**Headline:** Error #5: Missing Critical Fields

**Body Text:**
15% of customer records have no purchase date.
10% have no purchase amount.
20% have no contact information.

When you calculate RFM, these records are dead weight. They skew your analysis and can't be targeted.

Fix: Identify records with >50% missing data. Archive them or clean them. Takes 60 minutes.

---

**Headline:** Why Customers Drop Segments

**Body Text:**
Sometimes it's your fault:
- They're not satisfied with product/service
- They had a bad experience
- A competitor has better offer
- Your pricing changed and it's now uncompetitive

Sometimes it's not your fault:
- Their business is slowing
- They're consolidating suppliers
- Market conditions have changed
- They're restructuring internally

Knowing which is crucial. It changes your response.

---

### SLIDE 4: The Real Cost
**Headline:** What This Drop Actually Costs

**Body Text:**
One customer dropping from Champions to At-Risk isn't a small problem.

**Body Text:**

Champions → Loyals (warning: watch)
Loyals → Needing Attention (warning: intervene)
Needing Attention → At-Risk (emergency: save now)

When you spot this movement, you reach out. You understand why. You take corrective action.

Most companies find these patterns 60-90 days after they've started.

3. Propose a solution that addresses their actual problem (not just a discount).

4. If it's a competitive issue, offer differentiated value, not lower price.

5. If it's a fit issue, acknowledge it. Sometimes customers should churn.
